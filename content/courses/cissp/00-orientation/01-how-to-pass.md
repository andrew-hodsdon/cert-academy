## The loop

1. **Read a domain module.** Mark each lesson complete as you go.
2. **Take that domain's quiz** (25 questions drawn at random from the domain bank, 35 minutes). Review every miss; the explanation says why each wrong option is wrong.
3. **Repeat the quiz until you hold 80%.** Each attempt draws questions you have seen least first, so retakes are not memory tests.
4. When every domain is at 80%, sit the **full practice exam**: 125 questions in 3 hours, weighted like the real exam.
5. You are **exam-ready** when your last three full practice exams average **80% or more**. The readiness card on the course page tracks this. Then book.

The sidebar's *accuracy by domain* shows where your points leak. Go back to that module before the next full exam.

## How the real exam behaves

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT), 100–150 items, 3 hours |
| Unscored items | 25 pretest items hide inside the first 100; you cannot tell which |
| Navigation | No going back. Once you answer, it is final |
| Passing | 700/1000 standard; CAT stops when it is confident you are above or below it |
| Weights | D1 16%, D2 10%, D3 13%, D4 13%, D5 13%, D6 12%, D7 13%, D8 10% |

> [!TIP]
> Pace at about **1 minute 10 seconds per question**. If the exam stops at 100, that is not a sign of failure; it ends as soon as it is sure either way.

## Think like a manager

The CISSP tests judgement. When two answers look right, the better one usually:

- protects **people first** (life safety beats every asset);
- comes **earlier in the process** (assess risk, get management buy-in, write policy before buying a tool);
- is **complete** rather than a technical fix to one symptom;
- reflects **due care and due diligence**, and is something the business can own.

> [!WARNING]
> Watch the qualifiers: **FIRST**, **BEST**, **MOST**, **PRIMARY**. "What should you do FIRST?" often has several correct actions in the options; only one comes first.

## Check yourself

1. Your last three full practice exams scored 76%, 84% and 83%. Are you exam-ready?
2. A question asks what the security manager should do FIRST after discovering an unpatched critical server. Why might "patch it immediately" be wrong?
3. Why should you not read anything into a CAT exam ending at question 100?

1. Yes: the average is 81%, at or above 80%.
2. The first step is usually to assess the risk and follow change management (or the incident process), not to make an untested change.
